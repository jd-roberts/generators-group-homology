# Localization at 2: toral vanishing and an explicit residual summand

Joshua Roberts research project · 29 October 2026

Let $\zeta=\zeta_7$, $K=\mathbb Q(\zeta)$, $A=\mathbb Z[1/7,\zeta]$, $B=A[1/2]$, $G=\mathrm{SL}_2(A)$, and $G'=\mathrm{SL}_2(B)$. Put $k=\mathbb F_7$. For units $a,b$ write

$$t_R(a,b)=\big[[D(a)\mid D(b)]-[D(b)\mid D(a)]\big]\in H_2(\mathrm{SL}_2(R);k),
\qquad D(a)=\operatorname{diag}(a,a^{-1}).$$

Use the same notation with a superscript $\mathbb Z$ for integral classes.

**Outcome.** The localization formula in the memorandum is valid. The selected class $t_A(1-\zeta,1-\zeta^2)$ is zero **already over A**. More generally the entire diagonal-torus image in $H_2(G;k)$ is zero. In the localized group, the diagonal-torus image is exactly two-dimensional. It is generated by two explicit classes of integral order seven, and it splits the localization quotient, compatibly with Galois action.

These statements do **not** prove $j_*=0$. If $T_B$ denotes the diagonal-torus image in $H_2(G';k)$, we obtain

$$\boxed{H_2(G';k)=\operatorname{im}j_*\oplus T_B,
\quad \dim T_B=2,\quad
\operatorname{im}j_* = \ker\big(H_2(G';k)\to K_2(B)/7\big).}$$

In particular any surviving source contribution is outside the entire toral image. The rank of $j_*$ remains undetermined, between zero and six using the published bound. The exact calculations below establish these deductions; whether the specialized toral statements merit a new publication is not yet settled by the literature review.

## 1. Independent localization check

### 1.1 The primes above 2 and explicit prime elements

Over $\mathbb F_2$,

$$\Phi_7(X)=(X^3+X+1)(X^3+X^2+1).$$

The cubics have no root in $\mathbb F_2$, so they are distinct irreducibles. Since $\mathcal O_K=\mathbb Z[\zeta]$ and 2 is unramified, there are precisely two primes above 2, with residue fields $\mathbb F_8$. Label them $\mathfrak q_1=(2,1+\zeta+\zeta^3)$ and $\mathfrak q_2=(2,1+\zeta^2+\zeta^3)$, and set

$$\pi_1=1+\zeta+\zeta^3,\qquad \pi_2=1+\zeta^2+\zeta^3.$$

Direct polynomial multiplication gives

$$\pi_1\pi_2=2\zeta^3,\qquad \pi_2=\zeta^3\overline{\pi_1}.$$

Thus their norms are equal and their product of norms is 64. Norms in this totally imaginary field are positive for nonzero elements; hence both norms are 8. Reduction modulo the two cubic factors shows that $\pi_i$ vanishes at $\mathfrak q_i$ and not at the other prime. Consequently $(\pi_i)=\mathfrak q_i$ and $v_{\mathfrak q_j}(\pi_i)=\delta_{ij}$. In particular

$$A[1/\pi_1,1/\pi_2]=B,\qquad
\pi_1^{-1}=\frac{\zeta^4\pi_2}{2},\quad
\pi_2^{-1}=\frac{\zeta^4\pi_1}{2}.$$

These explicit elements avoid any ambiguity about a principal-power hypothesis in the one-prime localization theorem.

### 1.2 Perfectness

The set of places for A consists of three complex places and the prime above 7, so it has cardinality four. The intermediate localization at $\pi_1$ and the ring B have five and six places, respectively. Hutchinson's Proposition 2.2(1), using Vaserstein–Liehl, gives elementary generation for all three groups. No Euclidean-domain assumption is used.

The element $\lambda=\zeta$ is a unit, as is $\lambda^2-1$: each $1-\zeta^i$, $1\leq i\leq6$, has norm 7 and is supported above the inverted prime 7. For $x(r)=E_{12}(r)$,

$$D(\lambda)x(r)D(\lambda)^{-1}=x(\lambda^2r),\qquad
[D(\lambda),x(r/(\lambda^2-1))]=x(r).$$

Here $[g,h]=ghg^{-1}h^{-1}$. The lower root group has the analogous formula, with $\lambda^{-2}-1$ also a unit. Therefore every elementary generator is a commutator and all three groups are perfect, integrally.

### 1.3 Exactness and coefficients

Hutchinson [H, Theorem 5.17] applies to a ring $R=\mathcal O_{K,S}$ with $|S|\geq2$, a nonzero prime $\mathfrak q$, an element $x$ with $\mathfrak q^m=xR$, and a unit $\lambda$ for which $\lambda^2-1$ is a unit. Its conclusion is an exact sequence

$$H_2(\mathrm{SL}_2(R);\mathbb Z)\to H_2(\mathrm{SL}_2(R[1/x]);\mathbb Z)
\to\kappa(\mathfrak q)^\times\to0.$$

The boundary agrees with stabilization followed by the tame symbol. Corollary 5.20 handles several new places. All hypotheses hold here by 1.1–1.2, with $x=\pi_i$ and $m=1$ at each step. Therefore

$$H_2(G;\mathbb Z)\xrightarrow{j_{\mathbb Z}}H_2(G';\mathbb Z)
\xrightarrow{\delta_{\mathbb Z}} C_7\oplus C_7\to0.\tag{1}$$

There is no assertion that the first arrow is injective. Naturality of universal coefficients gives, for $H=G,G'$,

$$0\to H_2(H;\mathbb Z)\otimes k\to H_2(H;k)
\to \operatorname{Tor}_1^{\mathbb Z}(H_1(H;\mathbb Z),k)\to0.$$

The Tor group is zero by perfectness. Tensoring the **right-exact** sequence (1) with k is right exact, yielding

$$H_2(G;k)\xrightarrow{j_*}H_2(G';k)\xrightarrow{\delta}k^2\to0.\tag{2}$$

No general left-exactness of tensor products is used. It follows that

$$\dim_k H_2(G';k)=2+\operatorname{rank}j_*\in[2,8],\tag{3}$$

where only the upper endpoint uses Roberts's published $\dim H_2(G;k)\leq6$. The theorem's coefficient field is not being confused with the residue characteristic 2.

## 2. Why Steinberg equations give actual homological relations here

This step is essential. Vanishing in stable $K_2$ alone would not prove a cycle zero in unstable SL₂ homology.

**Lemma 1 (unique splitting over a root group).** If $V$ is an abelian group on which multiplication by an odd prime p is bijective, every central extension of V by $\mathbb F_p$ splits uniquely.

**Proof.** Its preimage E is nilpotent of class at most two. Its commutator pairing is bilinear on V, with values in the exponent-p kernel. Thus $\beta(v,w)=\beta(v/p,w)^p=1$, so E is abelian. For $v\in V$ choose any lift y of $v/p$ and define $s(v)=y^p$. Changing y by a kernel element does not change $y^p$. Since E is abelian this defines a homomorphism lifting V. Two splittings differ by a homomorphism $V\to\mathbb F_p$, necessarily zero by p-divisibility. ∎

**Lemma 2 (toral Steinberg relation).** For a commutative ring R with $1/p\in R$, and units $a,1-a$, the class $t_R(a,1-a)$ is zero in $H_2(\mathrm{SL}_2(R);\mathbb F_p)$.

**Proof.** Consider any central extension of $\mathrm{SL}_2(R)$ by $\mathbb F_p$. Lemma 1 supplies unique homomorphic lifts $X_{12}(r),X_{21}(r)$ of its two root subgroups. They satisfy additivity. Put

$$W_{ij}(u)=X_{ij}(u)X_{ji}(-u^{-1})X_{ij}(u).$$

Conjugation by this word gives a homomorphic lift of the opposite root subgroup with the usual parameter $-u^{-2}r$; uniqueness in Lemma 1 makes the Weyl conjugation relation exact. Also $W_{ij}(u)^{-1}=W_{ij}(-u)$ by additivity. Thus the defining relations of the rank-one Steinberg group in [H, Section 3.1] hold in the extension, and there is a homomorphism from $\mathrm{St}(2,R)$ to it over SL₂.

In $\mathrm{St}(2,R)$ put $h(u)=w_{12}(u)w_{12}(-1)$ and $c(u,v)=h(u)h(v)h(uv)^{-1}$. The established ring-level identities [H, Proposition 3.5(1),(5)] give

$$c(a,1-a)=c(1-a,a)=1.$$

Consequently the lifts $h(a)$ and $h(1-a)$ commute: their commutator is $c(a,1-a)c(1-a,a)^{-1}$. Evaluating an extension cocycle on the two-term toral cycle gives exactly this commutator, additively in $\mathbb F_p$. Every cohomology class evaluates to zero. Since cohomology over a field is dual to homology, these evaluations separate $H_2$, proving the assertion. ∎

This proof does **not** assert that $\mathrm{St}(2,R)\to\mathrm{SL}_2(R)$ is universally central or that its kernel is generated by symbols. Only its presentation and the cited symbol identities are used. Those identities were checked against an enlarged rendering of [H], p.7; the assertion there is for commutative rings, not just fields. Perfectness is not needed in Lemma 2.

For any abelian unit subgroup the toral pairing is alternating and bilinear on its mod-p H₁ coordinates. One explicit bar identity, for commuting g,h,k, is

$$\begin{aligned}
P(g,h,k)&=-[g\mid h\mid k]+[g\mid k\mid h]-[k\mid g\mid h],\\
\partial P(g,h,k)&=t(gh,k)-t(g,k)-t(h,k).
\end{aligned}\tag{4}$$

This is an identity of cycles/chains before passing to classes, with $t(g,h)=[g\mid h]-[h\mid g]$. The checker independently verifies (4). A factor -1 contributes zero mod 7 because it has order two. Lemma 2 therefore turns each exact unit equation $x+y=1$ into the relation $\log(x)\wedge\log(y)=0$ in the toral image, where logs here mean multiplicative exponent coordinates, not analytic logarithms.

## 3. The selected cycle is already zero in A

Write $u=1-\zeta$, $v=1-\zeta^2$, $w=1-\zeta^3$. In addition to $\zeta^i+(1-\zeta^i)=1$, $i=1,2,3$, the following are exact equations of A-units:

$$\begin{array}{c|c|c}
&x&1-x\\\hline
E_1&-w^2/(u^2v^2)&-\zeta^5v/(u^2w)\\
E_2&-\zeta/u^2&-\zeta^6/(vw)\\
E_3&\zeta^5w^2/u^2&\zeta^2vw/u^2
\end{array}\tag{5}$$

For example, clearing denominators reduces these to

$$w^3+\zeta^5v^3+u^2v^2w=0,\quad
u^2vw+\zeta vw+\zeta^6u^2=0,\quad
\zeta^5w^2+\zeta^2vw-u^2=0.$$

Each is verified by ordinary integer polynomial division by $\Phi_7$; the quotient certificates are recorded in `checked-toral-identities.json`.

In the coordinate order $(\zeta,u,v,w)$ and exterior order $(01,02,03,12,13,23)$, the three elementary equations followed by $E_1,E_2,E_3$ give the rows

$$R=\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0&0&0&0\\0&2&0&0&0&0\\0&0&3&0&0&0\\
3&3&4&1&6&0\\5&6&6&2&2&0\\1&5&1&5&2&5
\end{pmatrix}.$$

Its integer determinant is -300, congruent to 1 modulo 7. All six exterior-product directions are therefore zero in $H_2(G;k)$ by Lemma 2. Specifically,

$$e_u\wedge e_v=6R_1+2R_2+4R_4+2R_5\quad\text{in }k^6,\tag{6}$$

so the particular cycle in the question vanishes. Its image in B vanishes by naturality. This is stronger than merely proving its stable Steinberg symbol is zero.

For completeness, the standard unit description at prime 7 is $A^\times=\langle-1,\zeta,u,v,w\rangle\cong C_{14}\times\mathbb Z^3$; see [A, Section 4.2] specialized to this case. Thus the six directions just considered form the exterior part of torus H₂. Its remaining mod-7 summand is the cyclic-group H₂ from $C_{14}$. Conjugation by $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ inverts the diagonal torus but acts trivially on ambient homology. Inversion acts by -1 on that cyclic H₂ summand: its natural Bockstein is an isomorphism onto the 7-primary H₁ torsion. Since 2 is invertible in k, its ambient image is zero too. Hence

$$\operatorname{im}\big(H_2(A^\times;k)\xrightarrow{D_*}H_2(G;k)\big)=0.\tag{7}$$

**Boundary status.** The proof establishes that a finite mod-7 bar 3-chain filling the selected cycle exists. It does not exhibit that full 3-chain. Formula (4) supplies the bilinearity part of such a filling. The missing constructive ingredient is an explicit filling for each toral Steinberg relation in Lemma 2, replacing the central-extension/duality proof. No claim that an explicit localized filling was found is made. This remaining chain-extraction problem is distinct from the now-settled vanishing question.

## 4. Explicit nonzero classes in B and a Galois-equivariant splitting

Define

$$e_i=t_B^{\mathbb Z}(\zeta,\pi_i)\in H_2(G';\mathbb Z),\qquad i=1,2.$$

Their orders divide seven by integral bilinearity and $\zeta^7=1$. Under stabilization, the toral commutator corresponds to

$$\{\zeta,\pi_i\}-\{\pi_i,\zeta\}=2\{\zeta,\pi_i\}\in K_2(B).$$

Use Hutchinson's tame-symbol convention

$$\tau_{\mathfrak q}\{a,b\}=(-1)^{v(a)v(b)}a^{-v(b)}b^{v(a)}\pmod{\mathfrak q}.$$

In each residue field use the reduction of $\zeta$ as generator of $\mathbb F_8^\times$. The valuations in 1.1 give

$$\delta_{\mathbb Z}(e_1)=(-2,0),\qquad \delta_{\mathbb Z}(e_2)=(0,-2)\quad\text{in }(\mathbb Z/7)^2.\tag{8}$$

Both classes therefore have order **exactly** seven, and are independent, integrally and mod 7. The assignment of the standard residue generators to $3e_1,3e_2$ splits (1), since $3(-2)=1\pmod7$. We have an explicit decomposition of abelian groups

$$H_2(G';\mathbb Z)=\operatorname{im}j_{\mathbb Z}\oplus\langle e_1,e_2\rangle,
\qquad\langle e_1,e_2\rangle\cong(C_7)^2.\tag{9}$$

This provides representatives for the entire known residue contribution, not only an abstract lower bound.

For $\sigma(\zeta)=\zeta^3$ a generator of $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)$, direct calculation gives

$$\sigma(\pi_1)=\pi_2,\qquad\sigma(\pi_2)=\zeta^6\pi_1.$$

Bilinearity and alternation imply $\sigma(e_1)=3e_2$, $\sigma(e_2)=3e_1$. Hence (9), and its mod-7 version, split as Galois modules. On the explicit summand the matrix is $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&3\\3&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$; its eigenvalues are 3 and 4. Reversing tame-symbol convention changes both signs in (8) but none of these conclusions.

### The whole toral image in B is exactly this summand

The B-unit group is generated by $-1,\zeta,u,v,w,\pi_1,\pi_2$. Indeed adjoining the two principal primes extends the A-unit group by their two valuation directions. Its exterior H₂ source has dimension $\binom62=15$.

The certificate file records 13 exact equations $x+(1-x)=1$ using these units. Clearing their Laurent denominators gives integer polynomials divisible by $\Phi_7$. Their 13 exterior rows are independent over k. The independent checker computes this rank and verifies that every row lies in the kernel of the following tame matrix (columns in lexicographic exterior order for $(\zeta,u,v,w,\pi_1,\pi_2)$):

$$L=\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&0&5&0&0&0&1&0&0&2&0&5&0&1\\
0&0&0&0&5&0&0&0&4&0&0&1&0&3&2
\end{pmatrix}.\tag{10}$$

The residue logarithms of $(\zeta,u,v,w)$ are $(1,3,6,1)$ at $\mathfrak q_1$ and $(1,5,3,2)$ at $\mathfrak q_2$. The other prime generator has residue logarithm 4 at the first prime and 6 at the second. These values are independently verified in the two cubic quotients over $\mathbb F_2$; they determine (10) via twice the tame symbol.

Lemma 2 implies that the toral image has dimension at most $15-13=2$. Equation (8) gives the opposite inequality. The cyclic Bockstein contribution vanishes by the same Weyl argument as for A. Thus the **full** toral image is precisely $\langle e_1,e_2\rangle_k$. In particular

$$T_B\cap\operatorname{im}j_*=0.\tag{11}$$

This is a positive certificate argument. An unsuccessful search would not justify the upper bound; here all 13 specific unit equations and their independent row rank are checked. No assertion that a finite list exhausts all S-unit equations is needed.

Making 2 a unit is therefore consequential: it makes both $\pi_i$ units and allows the nonzero cycles above. It also permits $u/2+(1+\zeta)/2=1$, giving

$$t_B(u,v)=2t_B(u,2)-t_B(v,2).$$

The left side is already zero by Section 3. Such new equations constrain mixed cycles; they do not make every cycle zero. In fact (8) proves explicit nonvanishing in B.

## 5. Stable K-theory identifies exactly what remains unknown

There is no stable 7-primary source obstruction. Here is a verification independent of numerical tame-kernel tables.

The discriminant of K is $-7^5$. Minkowski's class-group bound is

$$M_K=(4/\pi)^3(6!/6^6)\sqrt{7^5}<5.$$

For an entirely rational check, use $\pi>3$ and $\sqrt{7^5}<130$; the resulting upper bound $\frac{64}{27}\frac{720}{46656}130$ is less than five. There are no prime ideals of norm 2, 3, or 4: the residue degree over 2 is three and that over 3 is six. Every ideal class thus contains the unit ideal, so $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal O_K)=0$, and also $\operatorname{Pic}(A)=\operatorname{Pic}(B)=0$.

Tate's theorem for S-integers with $1/7$ and $\mu_7$, as stated in [W, Theorem 4], identifies $K_2(R)/7$ with étale H² and gives the Picard–Brauer sequence. The Brauer invariants in [W, (5.6)] give $\operatorname{Br}(A)=0$: there are no real places and just one inverted finite place. Thus $K_2(A)/7=0$. For B there are three inverted finite places, so the same sequence gives $\dim K_2(B)/7=2$. Alternatively stable localization and $K_2(A)/7=0$ show that its two-component tame map is an isomorphism.

The compatibility in Hutchinson's localization theorem therefore gives a commutative square whose bottom arrow is an isomorphism:

$$\begin{array}{ccc}
H_2(G';k)&\xrightarrow{\delta}&k^2\\
\downarrow\mathrm{stab}&&\Vert\\
K_2(B)/7&\xrightarrow{\sim}&k^2.
\end{array}$$

It follows from (2) that

$$\operatorname{im}j_* = \ker(\mathrm{stab}).\tag{12}$$

Thus the target dimension-two question is exactly **mod-7 injectivity of stabilization for B**. It does not require an integral isomorphism with $K_2(B)$. Stable K-theory alone cannot establish this injectivity. Also, (12) is a statement about a mod-7 kernel: it should not be replaced without proof by the mod-7 tensor of an integral kernel.

Hutchinson's Theorem 6.10 and the recent [APRV, Theorem 3.13 and Remark 3.14] guarantee comparison after some sufficiently large enlargement; neither specifies that adjoining 2 suffices here. The term “totally imaginary ring” in [APRV, p.4] excludes the case $S=S_\infty$ with only complex places, not our localized ring. Modern semilocal comparison theorems in [MT] do not apply directly, since B has infinitely many maximal ideals. Elementary generation and a large minimum residue field do not by themselves provide the missing stability theorem.

## 6. From individual classes to the entire map

The six corrected classes on the verification branch remain only a candidate spanning set. The second is the toral commutator $u_1u_2u_1^{-1}u_2^{-1}$ under the verified convention, so Section 3 proves this class zero. **Conditionally on the six classes spanning**, at most five need further consideration, giving a source upper bound five and target upper bound seven. That conditional improvement is not asserted as an established bound here.

Without that premise, the mod-7 exponent kernel for the full 14-generator/64-relator presentation gives 50 formal generators mapping onto source H₂. A verified 6-generator/32-relator model gives 26. Toral classes can now be discarded homologically, but the remaining formal relation identities must still be justified. Dimension counts alone do not show that all residual classes are toral or symbol-generated.

Galois symmetry is now useful concretely: it preserves $\operatorname{im}j_*$, and Section 4 gives a known equivariant complement with characters 3 and 4. It does not determine the characters in the unknown summand. Its order six is prime to seven, so projectors can separate any residual calculation into characters once the action is known. The formal exterior module of the A-unit group is **not** a spanning model of source H₂; its actual ambient image is zero by (7).

A spectral sequence from the action on the valuation trees proves the localization sequence but does not automatically kill the left image. The exactness of that sequence identifies the missing contribution as a kernel, rather than supplying its vanishing. A structural proof that stabilization is mod-7 injective for this particular B would bypass the historical completion, but has not been obtained here.

## 7. Unit coinvariants: theoretical assessment only

Let $U=A^\times$ act by conjugation with $\operatorname{diag}(a,1)$. Squares act by inner automorphisms, since $\operatorname{diag}(v^2,1)=vI\,D(v)$. Hence the action on $M=H_2(G;k)$ factors through $U/U^2\cong(C_2)^4$. The four independent square classes can be taken as $-1,u,v,w$; $\zeta$ is itself a square.

Maschke's theorem decomposes M into 16 simultaneous sign-character summands. Its coinvariants are precisely its all-plus summand. With $T_1,\dots,T_4$ the involutions, the projector is

$$P_+=\frac1{16}(1+T_1)(1+T_2)(1+T_3)(1+T_4).$$

It suffices to prove $P_+=0$ on a justified spanning model, or to construct an equivariant surjection from a finite module with zero all-plus summand. A certified action on five remaining corrected classes would suffice **if** their spanning property is certified. Proving a single involution acts as -1 on every class would also suffice, but is not established.

For the split determinant extension and perfect G, the base-row differentials are zero by the section; thus the potentially displayed transgression vanishes. The degree-two GL₂ comparison is controlled by these coinvariants, as in the memorandum. Section 3 does not solve that problem: the unit action is on all of M, whereas the toral image is already zero. Galois characters cannot be substituted for unit square-class characters.

The localization problem now has explicit known classes and an identified unstable kernel, making it a clearer theorem target. The coinvariant problem may still be easier because only one character must be killed. At present neither has a proved decisive structural reduction. No search or substantial computation of this order-16 action was initiated.

## 8. Novelty, limitations, and the next proof attempt

**Established inputs:** Hutchinson's localization and symbol identities, Vaserstein–Liehl elementary generation, the published source bound, Tate's comparison, and the standard cyclotomic unit description. **Rigorous deductions here:** Lemmas 1–2 as applied above; the explicit A toral vanishing certificate; order-seven classes $e_i$ and integral/Galois splitting; the full B toral-image calculation; and (12). **Unresolved:** the rank of $j_*$, the nonzero/zero status of the remaining corrected candidates, a certified small spanning family, and the coinvariants of M. **Not supplied:** a full explicit bar 3-chain for the selected cycle. Its vanishing nevertheless has a proof independent of finding such a chain.

The searches performed were exact unit-equation enumerations only: 1,750 A units, 8,750 units from the subgroup generated by A-units and 2, and 3,402 B units from exponents -1,0,1 on $u,v,w,\pi_1,\pi_2$. Their discovery records used 30-second external wall limits, 25-second CPU limits and 512 MiB address-space limits; recorded wall times were below one second and peak memory below 16 MiB. No larger retry, GAP computation, completion, or general rewriting implementation occurred. The retained proof requires only checking 19 unit equations, not repeating these searches.

Literature checks included [H], [A], [AR], [R09], [R20], [W], and recent [APRV], [MT], [MRV], with targeted searches for the prime-7 toral obstruction and the localized cyclotomic group through 29 October 2026. No exact rank or dimension theorem for this B was located. The toral argument is built from standard Steinberg technology, and a first-priority claim for its specialization is **not** justified solely by these searches. Before treating it as a paper, compare it carefully with earlier computations of homological symbols and unit-symbol presentations, including the references to Suslin and Cohn in [H]. The full Anton conjecture includes higher obstruction degrees and is not proved here.

The present results could support a short note if this explicit degree-two prime-7 toral vanishing and the localized toral calculation have not appeared. A stronger research paper still calls for the unstable residual summand, or a new general criterion controlling it. The exact dimension-two theorem remains open in this work, and should not be treated as the expected answer.

**Single recommended next proof attempt:** take the first corrected source class $h_1$ at the pinned verification snapshot and attempt to show that its localized image is **toral**, using the unique root lifts of Lemma 1 and explicit identities involving $\pi_1,\pi_2$. Its tame boundary is automatically zero, so (11) would then force that image to vanish. This is a definite sufficient target and does not require the six-class spanning assertion. The exact input is the first entry of `docs/evidence/prime7-b0-corrected-words.g` at commit `515b306e7cb6a695724497447b85b05d6ab0c2fe`; membership/cycle status is reported certified there. Before using the word in a new calculation, replay its own finite membership and cycle certificate. This targets one genuinely residual class, rather than spending further effort on the toral cycle now proved zero. A success for one class still would not prove $j_*=0$ without control of a spanning family.
